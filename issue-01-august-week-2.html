This month’s issue gave you three questions to run on every student before your first meeting. This week we walk each one all the way down — what you’re actually looking for, why it matters, and what to do with the answer.

Fifteen minutes a student. No meeting required. Nobody’s permission needed.

01 This Week’s Focus · Question One

How much time is left?

Count school years, not birthdays. A student who turns 21 in October and one who turns 21 in June are a year apart in planning terms, and the file will not do that math for you.

Districts differ. Some students exit on their 22nd birthday; others finish the school year in which they turn 22. That single policy detail can be worth an entire program year.

Write down a projected exit date — an actual month and year — and confirm with your SELPA how your district handles a student who ages out mid-year. Then plan backward from that date. A student with fourteen months left needs a fundamentally different fall than one with three years.

Fourteen months is not “a little less time.” It is a different plan.

02 Question Two

Diploma or certificate?

The highest-stakes question of the three, and the one families understand least.

California programs use several course-of-study options — standard diploma, alternative diploma, certificate of completion — and the choice has consequences that outlast the paperwork.

The part families miss

Accepting a regular diploma ends IDEA eligibility. The day the student takes it, their time at TRACE ends — even if they have years of age eligibility left on paper. A certificate of completion does not end eligibility. Both are legitimate choices. Neither should be a surprise.

Two things to check while you have the file open:

  • Has the tradeoff actually been explained to this student and family, in plain language, and is that documented anywhere? “It was covered at the IEP” is not the same as understood.
  • Who gets a Summary of Performance. Federally it is required for students who exit with a regular diploma or by aging out — not for certificate exits or dropouts. Many districts write one for everybody anyway, which is the better practice. Find out which yours does before spring.
03 Question Three

What have they done in the real world?

One word does the work here: paid.

5.1× more likely to be employed after school Students who held a paid job at the time of exit, compared with those who did not. It is the strongest single predictor in the transition research — no other individual predictor comes close.

So when you read the work history, sort it honestly. Campus jobs, unpaid internships, and volunteer placements build real skills and belong in the plan — but they are not the thing the evidence points at, and as we covered last week, they do not count toward Indicator 14 either.

Then close the file and ask the student. Files record what the program arranged. Students know about the summer job at the uncle’s shop that nobody wrote down.

    04 Worked Example
    The tool above is preloaded with Marcus. Change one answer at a time and watch the fall plan move.

    Marcus, 20 — third year in the program

    Time left
    Turns 22 in March 2028 — call it two more program years.
    Track
    Certificate of completion. Mom has been asking lately whether he could still “get the real diploma.”
    On record
    Two years in the campus coffee cart. Unpaid. Nothing else documented.

    What the check surfaces: Marcus looks fine on paper and is quietly at risk. Two years of runway is real time — but he has zero paid work on record, which is the one variable most tied to where he lands. And the diploma question is live right now, not at 21, because switching tracks takes coursework and coursework takes semesters.

    Fall plan, in order: get the diploma-versus-certificate conversation on the calendar with Mom this month. Move him off the coffee cart into a paid community placement by November. Ask him what he did last summer — the file is thin, and thin files are usually incomplete rather than empty.

    Try this week

    Ninety minutes. Whole caseload.

    One line per student — exit date, track, paid work yes or no. Then sort them into three piles:

    • Under two years left and no paid work — these are your fall.
    • Track decision unresolved — these are your first family calls.
    • Everyone else — steady, revisit in January.
