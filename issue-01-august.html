Welcome back. Your students are 18 to 22 — still eligible, still on the roster, still building. That extra time is the whole opportunity: a chance to finish a diploma, get real work experience, and line up what comes next before they exit.

This newsletter is your monthly resource for evidence-based practices you can use with students this week — not just read about. Each issue, one topic. Let’s start with the why.

01 This Month’s Focus

Every student will exit.
Where they land is the point.

For your TRACE students, “exit” is close — many age out at 22. So the runway is now.

One year after a student leaves, the state checks in on them. The formal name is Indicator 14, but you can just call it what it is: where did they end up? The answer gets sorted into three tiers — and each one is wider than the last.

Indicator 14 is one of the federal State Performance Plan indicators every state reports under IDEA. It is a census of leavers, not a sample — more on that in Week 1.
14A
In school — the good kind

Enrolled in higher education: college, trade school, or a university program.

14B
All of 14A — or working a real job

Competitively employed: regular hours, at or above minimum wage.

14C
All of 14B — or any other training or paid work

Adult ed, apprenticeship, short-term training, or other paid work.

They stack. A student in college is counted in 14A and 14B and 14C. That means 14C is the widest measure of “landed somewhere” — not the bad outcome. The bad outcome is the group that never shows up in any of them.

NONE
Counted in none of the three

Not working, not in school or training. Often called “not engaged.”

The whole job this year: move students up into 14A or 14B, and make sure nobody finishes the year outside 14C entirely.

Everything else in this newsletter comes back to that

Work experience, diploma completion, soft skills, family support — every topic we’ll cover this year is a lever on that one sentence.

Under IDEA, transition planning is required — but the law is a floor, not a ceiling. Compliance isn’t the goal; a better life is. California districts report this data to CDE every year. Curious where your program landed? Ask your SELPA or special ed coordinator. Knowing your starting point is step one.

02 Practice Spotlight

The Runway Check

Fifteen minutes per student. Do it before your first meeting.

Pull each student’s file and answer three questions. For TRACE students, the clock is the thing you can’t ignore.

  1. 1
    How much time is left?

    How many years of eligibility does this student have before they age out?

  2. 2
    Diploma or certificate?

    Which track are they on — and do the student and family understand the difference and what each opens up?

  3. 3
    What have they done in the real world?

    What work or community experience is already on record?

This tells you who needs the most groundwork this year. Don’t wait for the IEP meeting to ask.

03 Resource of the Month
Careful: the site holds two different tools that people constantly mix up. Week 3 sorts them out.
  • Free
  • transitionta.org
  • Federally funded

NTACT:C — the field’s “what actually works” library

The federally-funded technical assistance center for secondary transition. It keeps the most complete database of evidence-based practices in the field — sorted by how strong the evidence is, and tied to the Taxonomy for Transition Programming 2.0.

Bookmark it. When you’re not sure whether a strategy is worth your time, this is where you check.

Do this month

Ask one question out loud.

At your first team meeting: “Where did last year’s leavers actually end up?” If nobody knows, that’s your first assignment — request it from your SELPA.

You can’t move a number you’ve never seen.
